Mercedes-Benz E350

Oviedo, Florida · Black Mercedes-Benz E350 · Multi-stage paint correction and ceramic coating

Project overview

  • Vehicle: Mercedes-Benz E350
  • Color: Black
  • Location: Oviedo, Florida
  • Services: Multi-stage paint correction and ceramic coating
  • Recorded condition: Moderate to severe swirl marks, marring, and water spots
  • Goal: Restore gloss and clarity, then protect the prepared finish

The condition before correction

The original project notes describe a finish that had lost clarity under direct light. Improper washing had left visible swirl marks, while mineral deposits had created water spots across multiple panels. The vehicle could look clean from a distance, yet the defects became more obvious when light reflected across the black paint.

Because a ceramic coating preserves the surface beneath it rather than correcting defects, the paint needed to be assessed and refined before protection was applied.

Wash and decontamination

The process began with a thorough exterior wash and preparation to remove loose dirt and contamination. Proper preparation helps prevent remaining debris from being dragged across the paint during polishing.

Multi-stage paint correction

Professional polishing equipment, compounds, and finishing polishes were used in a multi-stage process to improve the visible swirls and refine the finish. Each panel was inspected during the work. The aim was to improve gloss and reflection while respecting the limits of the clear coat; not every deep mark or etched spot can be safely removed.

Ceramic coating and final inspection

After correction and surface preparation, a ceramic coating was applied to the prepared paint. The final inspection checked the finish under appropriate lighting and reviewed the coating application. A coating can support hydrophobic behavior and easier maintenance, but it does not make paint scratch-proof or eliminate future washing.

Why correction comes before coating

If swirl marks or water-spot etching remain before coating, they may remain visible after the coating cures. When those defects matter to the owner and the paint can safely be polished, correction is the opportunity to improve the finish before protection is installed.
