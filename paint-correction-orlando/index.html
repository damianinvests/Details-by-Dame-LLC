The first step in the finish path

Correct the surface before you protect it.

Paint correction uses machine polishing to refine the clear coat and improve how light reflects from the finish. It is the service to consider when swirl marks, light scratches, water spots, oxidation, or haze make the paint look dull.

Every vehicle is different. We start with the condition of the finish and discuss the improvement that is realistic for your paint before work begins.

Paint correction FAQ

Before you book.

Is paint correction the same as waxing?

No. Wax adds a temporary protective layer. Paint correction is machine polishing that refines paint defects and improves clarity.

Should correction come before ceramic coating?

If the paint has defects you want improved, correction should happen before coating. A coating can leave existing marks visible beneath the protection.

Can you remove deep scratches?

Some light scratches may improve. Deep scratches may be too severe for safe polishing and need a different repair. We inspect first.

Inspection before correction

Mobile Paint Correction in Orlando

Paint correction should be based on the vehicle in front of us—not a preset package that assumes every clear coat is the same. Mobile paint correction may be possible when the vehicle is parked in a clean, covered or suitably shaded area with safe access, stable working conditions, and permission to work on-site. We confirm the location before scheduling because heat, direct sun, wind, dust, and site restrictions can affect the quality and safety of polishing.

Pre-paint inspection and paint gauge readings

We inspect the finish under suitable lighting, identify swirls, haze, water spotting, scratches, edges, and signs of previous repair where visible. Where the panel and substrate support it, a paint-thickness gauge helps identify inconsistent or unusually thin areas. Most gauges measure total coating thickness rather than clear coat alone; readings are a risk-assessment tool, not a precise map of remaining clear coat on every panel.

Before committing to a full vehicle, we perform a test spot on a representative area. The chosen pad and compound or polish are tested, then the result is inspected for defect reduction, gloss, and finish quality. This establishes a realistic target and helps avoid unnecessary clear-coat removal.

One-step, two-step, and multi-stage correction

One-step correction is a single polishing stage aimed at improving gloss and reducing lighter wash swirls and haze. Two-step correction generally uses a dedicated cutting stage followed by a refining polish. Multi-stage correction adds further refinement for demanding finishes, but more stages do not automatically mean a better or safer result.

Wet sanding is a specialized, higher-risk option for selected defects and suitable paint systems. It removes material and is not appropriate for every scratch or panel. If a defect is too deep to correct safely, leaving some of it visible is better than compromising the finish.

Decontamination and final preparation

Iron remover chemically targets embedded ferrous fallout. A lubricated clay bar can remove remaining bonded contaminants, but may cause light marring that should be inspected and polished as appropriate. After correction, an IPA-based panel wipe or dedicated coating-prep solution removes polishing oils and residue so the true finish can be evaluated and the surface can be prepared for protection.

Continue to ceramic coating when you are ready to protect the corrected finish.
