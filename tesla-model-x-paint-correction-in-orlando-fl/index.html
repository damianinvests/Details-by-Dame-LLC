Tesla Model X

Orlando, Florida · Multi-stage paint correction project

Project overview

  • Vehicle: Tesla Model X
  • Location: Orlando, Florida
  • Service recorded: Multi-stage paint correction
  • Recorded condition: Moderate swirl marks, light scratches, heavy water spotting, and reduced gloss
  • Goal: Restore gloss and improve paint clarity

What the inspection found

The original project story describes visible swirl marks, fine scratches, and significant water spotting across the paint and glass. Direct Florida sunlight made the defects easier to see and reduced the depth and clarity of the finish.

Wash and decontamination

The vehicle received a thorough wash followed by chemical and mechanical decontamination. The recorded preparation included a foam pre-wash, pH-balanced hand wash, iron-remover treatment, and clay-bar decontamination. The purpose was to remove loose dirt and bonded contamination before polishing, not to use clay as a substitute for correction.

Paint inspection and multi-stage polishing

Panels were inspected under suitable lighting to identify defect severity and areas that needed additional attention. A multi-stage machine-polishing process used a compounding stage followed by a finishing polish to improve the appearance of defects and restore gloss. The approach was selected with the integrity of the clear coat in mind.

The original project record estimated roughly 80–90% improvement in visible defects. That figure is a project-specific estimate from the original page, not a guarantee or a promise for another vehicle. Results vary with paint condition, defect depth, previous repairs, and available clear coat.

What paint correction can and cannot do

Correction can improve many wash-induced swirls, light scratches, and some water-spot marks. Deep scratches, severe etching, or damage through the clear coat may remain because removing too much material would be unsafe. A coating, if chosen as a separate next step, should be applied only after the finish has been properly prepared.
